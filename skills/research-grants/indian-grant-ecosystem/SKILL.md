# Indian Grant Ecosystem & Opportunity Selection

Grounded in a 2026 grant-writing workshop for early- and mid-career Indian
researchers. Two jobs: (1) read the Indian funding landscape realistically, and
(2) pick the *right* scheme for a specific idea before you spend a word writing.

## Hard rules

1. **No fabricated citations.** Every agency figure, call, or statistic you
   cite must resolve to a verifiable record (the agency's call PDF, a dated
   ISTIP/IndiaBioscience listing, or the DST/NRF report with a retrieval date).
   If a number cannot be re-sourced this cycle, label it "indicative, verify
   against the current call" rather than presenting it as current.
2. **No claim without provenance.** Every quantitative claim (acceptance rate,
   budget norm, eligibility limit) must point to a file, table, or URL the
   agent can show. "Trust me" is not provenance.
3. **No silent failure.** Every call-lookup (portal fetch, web search, PDF
   read) must declare what happened on non-zero — a stale or empty result is
   reported, not passed off as "no opportunities found."
4. **Distinguish the mandate from the agency.** An agency (e.g. NRF, VGST, DST,
   ICMR) hosts many schemes; the *scheme* carries the mandate, career-level
   filter, and subject filter. Eligibility is decided at the scheme level.
5. **Prefer agency-first discovery over portal-only.** Portals lag; a weekly
   agency-website sweep is the reliable early signal.

## When to use

- An early- or mid-career Indian researcher is choosing which call to target.
- You need to explain *why a given proposal is hard to fund* in India (landscape
  numbers) before committing to a scheme.
- Mapping a research idea onto a scheme by mandate, career level, and subject.
- Setting budget expectations before drafting (agency OIM/overhead norms).

## When NOT to use

- The proposal itself: write it with `grant-proposal-craft` (and `nih-r01` /
  `specific-aims` where a foreign scheme is actually the target).
- A pure fellowship only — use `fellowships`. A non-governmental funder — use
  `foundation-grants`.
- US/EU/UK schemes (NIH, ERC, NSF) — those have their own skills; this one is
  the Indian ecosystem.

## Prerequisites

- A concrete research idea (or at least a research area) with a named primary
  method or organism.
- The PI's career stage: years in post, age, and whether they have moved
  institution recently (eligibility for several schemes).
- Access to the internet to check the current call cycle (numbers below are
  directional; the call PDF is authoritative).

## Landscape reading (2026, indicative)

India's gross expenditure on R&D (GERD) crossed **0.8% of GDP for the first
time** in 2025–26, reaching ~**0.84%**, after being stuck near 0.7% for roughly
five years. Gross R&D spend ~₹2.4 lakh crore, but **extramural research grants
are only ~1.4% of that (~₹3,500 crore)** — the pool every researcher competes
for. Treat these as *directional 2026 figures* and re-source against the latest
DST/NRF report before quoting them in a document.

Acceptance is the binding constraint, not money:

| Scheme | Illustrative selectivity | What it signals |
|--------|--------------------------|-----------------|
| VGST (Karnataka, state) | <3% (~1-in-30) in a cited cycle | High competition, state-scoped |
| NRF — Advanced Research Grant (ARG) | 12,000+ applications in the most recent cycle | The most crowded core-research call in India |
| SERB / core science calls | Selectivity ranges ~2–22% across areas by year | Varies sharply by discipline |

(The old "Advanced Research Grant" core grant was rebranded; the receiving board
is subsumed under the newer NRF structure. Confirm the current name and call PDF
for the cycle you are targeting.)

**The takeaway the workshop drives home:** good science alone is not enough —
the applicant who presents their science more clearly to the funder wins. That
is the writing work of `grant-proposal-craft`.

## Scheme-selection workflow

1. **Name the agency universe for the idea.** Government by type:
   - **Central ministries** (e.g. MoES, MoE, Ayush, Electronics & IT, Mines,
     Posts) — ministry-specific programs.
   - **Departments** — DST, DBT, Department of Health Research (DHR).
   - **Autonomous bodies** — ICMR (under the Ministry of Health & Family
     Welfare), CSIR institutes, UGC/ANRF/NRF boards.
2. **Classify the funding purpose** (a scheme fits one bucket):
   research grant · fellowship (individual career) · infrastructure grant
   (institution facility) · conference grant (host) · travel grant (attend).
3. **Match mandate, not just agency.** For each candidate scheme read the call
   and ask: does my idea fit the *subject* mandate **and** the *career-level*
   mandate? Examples from the workshop:
   - **NRF Advanced Research Grant** — generic in *subject* (any SeT area) and
     generic in *career level* (emerging / mid / established).
   - **PMRG (Prime Minister's Research Grant)** — generic in subject, but
     *specific* to early career: within 2 years of a new institution **and**
     under ~42 years.
   - **A disease-specific BIRAC/ICMR call** (the workshop used a
     lymphatic-filariasis example) — a call can be far narrower than ARG.
   - If the closest published work is nearly identical to your idea, novelty
     is at risk — reconsider the framing before you apply.
4. **Check the travel line.** Some schemes carry no travel budget; a project
   built on field sampling or foreign collabs may be mismatched.
5. **Pre-award vs post-award.** Everything up to the award is pre-award
   (planning → identification → submission → review → award). After money
   lands it is post-award (implementation, reporting). The proposal quality is
   decided almost entirely in pre-award.
6. **Discovery routine.** Use **ISTIP** (India Science, Technology & Innovation
   Portal) and **IndiaBioscience** for a first sweep — but both lag the
   agency's own site. Keep a shortlist of the 3–5 agencies that fund your area
   and sweep their sites weekly; early call visibility is what buys you writing
   time.
7. **Read the norms before the budget.** Agency Office Instructions / circulars
   cap personnel, overhead, and contingency — and the rule is often buried
   (the workshop's warning: "page 11, para 3, line 2"). Indicative norms:
   - **NRF OIM** — up to ~10% overhead for academic institutions on a
     project up to ₹1 crore.
   - **ICMR** — ~3% staff + contingency (very low).
   Re-verify against the cycle's OIM; these figures change.

## Pitfalls

- **Choosing a scheme by money, not mandate.** A bigger ticket that does not
  fit the career-level or subject filter is a guaranteed rejection.
- **Trusting the portal as current.** Portal listings lag the call notification;
  the agency PDF is the source of truth.
- **Quoting landscape numbers as live.** The GERD/acceptance figures above are
  2026 indicators; re-source before putting them in a formal document.
- **Missing the travel line** — a no-travel scheme for a travel-heavy plan.
- **Ignoring the OIM caps** — a budget that exceeds the agency's personnel or
  overhead norm is auto-flagged in review.

## Validation

- Every scheme you shortlist is matched on *both* subject and career-level
  mandate, with the call PDF as evidence.
- Every landscape number carries a source + retrieval date, or is labelled
  indicative.
- The agency OIM caps have been read for the target call before the budget.
- At least one agency site was checked this cycle, not only the portal.
